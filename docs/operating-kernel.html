01  The kernel

I author the discipline, specify the verification, and own the kill calls — I don't write the code. The kernel is the part I own:

I built an operating kernel, and ported it.

Deltascanner was the genesis — distressed-property intelligence across thirteen markets, where the disciplines got forged against real data failures, not in theory.

Then I abstracted the intelligence-aggregation core into an intel loop: capture, compress, route, verify, on an append-only ledger — the loop that has since iterated into a launchpad. That loop is the kernel. MFL is instance #2 — the same kernel running in a domain it had nothing to do with. And it's live.

Origin

Deltascanner taught the load-bearing lesson: build verification that can disagree with the system, or it just ratifies the system's own bugs. And the honesty rules it forced — union-scoring across sources double-counts and inflates the parcel total (so you dedupe, never union), and a normalization pass can silently destroy granular data if the schema isn't guarded.

Kernel

The intel loop is the abstraction. The portable asset is the loop and the operator-trust discipline: the operator curates which signal is worth logging, the machine compresses and bounds it, an independent check verifies it, the append-only ledger keeps all of it. Domain-independent by construction.

Instance 02

MFL ported the kernel to a live, real-time decision domain — which was itself a test of the kernel. It surfaced edges Deltascanner never hit: a tie-collapse in the scoring bands, provenance lost when a pick is saved, an erosion-fade path. Each one hardened the loop.

The claim, in one line: computation is priced, curation is the edge, discipline is the proof. The rest walks instance #2 — including the part where I killed my own winner (§08).

Each modifier's magnitude is the operator's curated label, not an auto-measured share — the auto-measured path is exactly the one §08's gate killed, and it never runs here. And the moves aren't proportional: Burden's +7.45% buys 12 spots, Fannin's +3.93% only 3 — where a row already sits decides how far a lift carries it.

A lift is the operator expressing a view, not evidence the view beats the market. The ±15% bound (§05) embodies the same caution §08's gate independently confirmed: no single signal should swing the board far, because most edges are already priced.

04  The kernel's sharpest primitive

A test was built to disagree with the system — and it did.

This is the proof the kernel actually works, not just looks ported. The check rebuilds the number it's auditing from the raw log, independently — instead of trusting the system's own shortcut. That's what lets it disagree with the thing it's checking. On data the system had never seen, it did: it failed on its own, at a hard gate.

Had the check trusted the system's own shortcut instead of rebuilding the number, both would have been wrong the same way and the test would have passed against itself. Independence is the whole point — a test that can only agree isn't a test. (The failure is logged and shown, not hidden behind a green check.)

06  What MFL surfaced

Porting the kernel was a test of the kernel. Here's what it found.

The sharpest finding first: provenance — the trail of where a recommendation came from — survives scoring, but dies at the moment of action. The board row carries its trail; the saved Pick is a locked 6-field record that drops it. The cockpit reads the trail to rank, then saves a Pick stripped of it.

Dormant

Design-gap

The forward outcome loop is unbuilt. "Did the decision pay off" can only be earned forward — a track record, not a backtest. The close (§09) is exact about it.

These are static properties — true things about how the system is shaped right now, surfaced by the audit. The next section is different: it's an event.

07  The learning

The system reported a fact. I supplied the verdict.

The scoring step sorts every row into three bands by cutting the distribution into thirds. Early on, the scores tied at the cut line — the split collapsed and swept 251 of 252 rows into a single HIGH band. The board came back almost entirely one call. Nothing in the system flagged that as wrong; no rule fired.

I saw it, suspected over-concentration, and had a read-only diagnostic run that confirmed it and found the cause. The fix — a tie-breaking rule — dropped HIGH to 49.

This is the part the machine couldn't do: it measured a neutral fact (251 in one band) and had no way to know it was wrong. The verdict — "a decision surface can't be 99% one call" — came from the operator. That's the taste edge, and it's why this is a learning, not a catch: unlike the validator in §04, the system didn't catch itself. A reviewer will press on that difference. Naming it is the honest move.

08  The gate has teeth

I found a real signal, and killed it.

Third rung of one ladder: in §04 the system caught itself; in §07 I caught what the system couldn't; here I kill my own winner.

Before measuring anything, I committed to a pass bar in writing: an edge has to clear +1.5 on the outcome metric over its baseline, beat a random-chance null, and hold across at least three separate years. Then I ran that frozen gate four times against five held-out years it had never seen (2021–2025).

Three candidate edges failed clean — the market already prices them in. The fourth came back real: +0.997, better than chance, holding in all five years I could test, and robust to stress. I killed it anyway — because +0.997 is under the +1.5 bar I'd set before I looked.

bar pre-registered 70976a2, 2026-06-27 13:21 PT — run logged 32aeb89 15 min 12 sec later

A gate that only ever says yes is a rubber stamp; this one said no to a winner that wasn't big enough. Be exact about what that proves: the gate and the judgment — committing to the bar before measuring, judging "worth it" separately from "real," killing my own idea on the evidence. It rules out the automated edge (these formulas don't beat this market). It does not validate what ships — the forward track record starts in September. (This is a backward test against history; the forward outcome loop is the §09 seam — a different thing.)

09  Where the evidence stops

The backward question is answered. The forward one is the bet.

Two different questions hide under "did it work," and they don't have the same status. The backward one — does the mechanical edge hold up against history — has an answer: no. The gate ran it across five held-out years it had never seen (2021–2025) and the formula collapsed (§08). I ran that test against my own idea, and the result came back negative.

The forward question — does curated judgment, bounded and accrued over time, actually pay off against real outcomes — has no answer yet, and by its nature can't have one in advance. You can't pre-register a backtest for "was the operator right to log this"; its only proof is a track record, and that starts in September. So the outcome loop isn't a hedge that's "designed but not built" — it's the one stage that can only be earned forward. The forward record is the next stage for the kernel, not an MFL shortfall.

One operating kernel — forged on Deltascanner, abstracted into an intel loop, running now as instance #2. The mechanical edge is the one thing my own gate already ruled out; what's left is disciplined curation, bounded, with a forward record about to start accruing. Live, with real receipts, and honest about exactly which question it hasn't answered yet.

The portable asset is the loop and the discipline, not a computed edge. Instance #2 proves the loop ports; §08 proves the verification has teeth; September is where the only claim that can't be pre-tested — that the curation pays off — begins to earn its evidence.
